# Making Kaayra premium and professional

Method: a web search on how Forest Essentials and Kama Ayurveda are positioned (sources at the end), read against this repository. It is market commentary, not research into Kaayra's own customers. Nothing here has been tested with users.

## What the research says

1. **Context makes premium, not ingredients.** Forest Essentials "did not change Ayurveda, they changed the room it walked into": the same tradition placed in a luxury design context. A brand can feel trustworthy and effective and still not feel premium; both are needed.
2. **Avoid the "grandmother's recipe" tone.** Folksy, remedy-style language is what pulls Ayurvedic brands into the low-price trap.
3. **Efficacy and authenticity carry repeat custom.** Kama Ayurveda's CEO says it is not marketed as luxury; repeat buyers return because it works. For Kaayra, whose products are not yet approved, the honest equivalent today is *depth and transparency*, not claims.
4. **Premium is protected by where you appear.** Own website first; avoid mass marketplaces next to very cheap oils.
5. **Rituals, storytelling and one design language** across packaging, photography and website.
6. **Younger audiences** (from the earlier trend search) want a lighter, human, less corporate feel and confident typography, but muted palettes read as calm and trustworthy for wellness.

## Priorities

**Do now (no assets needed)**
- Apply the voice guide below to the homepage, story and approach pages.
- One typographic rhythm: one display size per page, generous space, no more than two accent devices per screen.
- Keep every screen to one primary action; the launch list is the conversion goal.
- Add a short "How we work" page structure: sourcing principles, review steps, what "in development" means. Only statements the company can stand behind.

**Needs assets from you (highest value first)**
1. Photography: three to five images on a warm neutral background, in a single light and colour grade (botanical macros, hands and materials, Kashi). One consistent treatment matters more than quantity.
2. Final logo set and a botanical K icon.
3. Founder portraits and a short real founder note.
4. A photographed packaging mock-up only once the label is regulator-approved.

**Needs business decisions**
- Distribution stance (own site first, no mass marketplaces) written into the Phase 5 plan.
- Price positioning, decided before any price is shown. Prices stay hidden until then.
- Who reviews copy for Ayurvedic claims before publication.

## Voice guide

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Calm, precise, specific: "Eight formulations in development" | Hype and adjectives: "amazing", "miracle", "ultimate" |
| Names and places: Kashi, Varanasi | Generic "ancient secrets", "grandmother's recipe", "natural goodness" |
| Statements of process: what is reviewed, by whom, when | Benefit or cure claims of any kind |
| Short sentences, one idea each | Exclamation marks, urgency, countdowns |
| "In development", "not for sale yet" | Implying availability, certification or approval |

"Ritual" language is common in premium wellness but borders on benefit claims; use it only for how a person uses something, never for what it does, and have it reviewed.

## Things not to do

Fake scarcity or reviews; discount-style badges; stock photos of people with perfect skin; emoji; a marketplace listing before the brand has its own anchor; heavy animation; pricing before positioning is decided.
